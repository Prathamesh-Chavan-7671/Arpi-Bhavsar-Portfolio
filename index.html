Case study

PCG 2026: Peptides & Complex Generics Symposium

The project

PCG 2026 brought together pharmaceutical industry leaders, regulatory experts, academicians, and technology specialists to examine the evolving landscape of peptides and complex generics. The programme needed to connect emerging science, manufacturing realities, regulatory considerations, and industry priorities in a coherent, industry-focused forum.

My contribution

I played a key role in conceptualising the symposium and translating its scientific and strategic priorities into a structured programme. My work spanned agenda development, senior speaker engagement, technical content preparation, stakeholder communications, and coordination of programme delivery.

  • Scientific programme strategy: Helped shape the scientific vision and thematic framework, connecting emerging scientific developments, technological advances, and industry priorities.
  • Agenda and session design: Led the development of plenary sessions, technical panels, leadership roundtables, and focused workshops across peptide therapeutics, oligonucleotides, complex generics, bioanalytical characterisation, formulation, drug delivery, and pharmaceutical manufacturing.
  • Speaker and stakeholder engagement: Managed identification, outreach, invitations, confirmations, follow-ups, and coordination with industry executives, regulatory representatives, academics, and international experts.
  • Technical content development: Prepared session briefs, moderator notes, discussion prompts, thematic frameworks, and speaker background documents to clarify session objectives and support substantive discussion.
  • Programme execution: Aligned speaker requirements, session schedules, stakeholder communications, and logistical needs across teams.
Impact and outputs

Helped create a structured forum for cross-sector discussion on the scientific, regulatory, and manufacturing challenges shaping the peptides and complex generics ecosystem. The programme connected multiple stakeholder groups through curated sessions and focused discussions. Post-conference, I am contributing to technical whitepapers that synthesise expert deliberations and translate session insights into longer-form knowledge resources.

Case study

Hands-on Training Workshop on Alternatives to Animal Models (3D Cell Culture)

The project

A four-day hands-on technical training programme designed to provide academic and industry participants with practical exposure to emerging alternatives to animal models, including 3D cell culture, 3D bioprinting, and organ-on-chip technologies.

My contribution

I led the planning, coordination, and execution of the programme, shaping a learning experience that combined scientific context with practical exposure.

  • Programme design: Developed the four-day structure around key advanced in-vitro technologies and their relevance to New Approach Methodologies (NAMs).
  • Learning flow and content: Integrated expert-led lectures, technical demonstrations, and practical sessions to connect foundational concepts with hands-on learning.
  • Faculty and technical coordination: Coordinated expert engagement, session schedules, training requirements, and access to relevant technologies, equipment, and technical expertise.
  • Participant coordination: Managed registration-related communications, participant queries, and coordination with academic and industry attendees.
  • On-ground delivery: Led coordination across all four days, overseeing faculty requirements, session transitions, practical activities, and participant engagement.
Impact and outputs

Delivered a structured four-day learning programme that brought together expert instruction, technical demonstrations, and practical training. The workshop supported scientific outreach and capability-building in advanced in-vitro models, helping participants engage with approaches being explored as alternatives to conventional animal-based research.

Case study · Organising Committee, Scientific Programmes & Communications

3DCC × Biologics 2026 Conference

The project

A multidisciplinary conference exploring advances at the intersection of 3D cell culture, biologics, and emerging biotechnology platforms. The programme required coordination across multiple scientific tracks, speakers, poster reviewers, and conference spaces.

My contribution

As part of the organising committee, I contributed to scientific programme development, speaker coordination, research poster evaluation, communications, and conference delivery.

  • Programme coordination: Contributed to developing and refining the scientific agenda across thematic tracks and parallel sessions.
  • Speaker engagement: Managed outreach, invitations, confirmations, follow-ups, and coordination with national and international subject matter experts.
  • Abstract screening and evaluation: Coordinated review of research poster abstracts for scientific relevance and alignment with conference themes.
  • Poster jury coordination: Led poster evaluation activities, coordinating jury members, co-chairs, and reviewers; managing poster allocations; and facilitating evaluation for Best Poster Awards.
  • Multi-track delivery: Coordinated programme requirements across four parallel scientific tracks and five conference halls, working with speakers, moderators, jury members, and organising teams.
  • Scientific communications and operations: Supported session content, speaker information, website and programme materials, announcements, poster presentations, and on-ground coordination.
Impact and outputs

Supported the delivery of a complex, multi-track scientific conference by coordinating programme requirements across four parallel tracks and five conference halls. The work also helped establish a structured process for poster abstract review and Best Poster Award evaluation, alongside speaker communications and scientific outreach.
